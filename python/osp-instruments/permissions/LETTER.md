# Permission request to rights holders (draft for the project owner)

Sent by the project owner (planning/question-bank.md, lead decision 5), never by an agent.
One letter per holder; fill in the bracketed parts. File every reply, yes or no, in this
folder as `<instrument-id>-<yyyy-mm-dd>.md` (the reply text, who sent it, the date), and
only then change the instrument's class to `author-permission` with `licence.permission`
naming that file.

---

**Subject:** Permission to include the [INSTRUMENT NAME] in an open-source survey tool for researchers

Dear [NAME],

I lead Open Survey Platform, a free, open-source survey builder for academic research
(MIT licence, [REPOSITORY URL]). Researchers build questionnaires in it and run them in
their own studies; the software ships with a small library of validated instruments so
that a researcher can add, for example, the PHQ-9 or the IPIP scales in one step, with the
citation attached.

We would like to include the **[INSTRUMENT NAME]** ([CITATION]) in that library, and we
are writing to ask your permission, because your terms page ([TERMS URL], read
[DATE]) [allows free use for research but does not mention redistribution in software /
limits use to non-commercial purposes / does not say whether the items may be reproduced
in other formats].

What we would include, and how:

- The English item wording, instructions and response options, **unchanged**, in a
  machine-readable file inside the software. The questions are displayed in our own
  survey layout (one question or grid per screen), not as a facsimile of your form.
- The scoring rule as a computed variable, as published in [SOURCE].
- Your citation, your copyright or permission line exactly as you specify, and a link to
  your page, shown to the researcher whenever the instrument is inserted and printed in
  the codebook of every study that uses it.
- Translations only if you tell us which ones we may include.

Please note the licence position honestly: the software is MIT-licensed, so anyone may
install, copy and redistribute it, including commercial organisations. Our users are
mostly university researchers, but we cannot technically limit who uses it. If you can
grant permission only for non-commercial use, please say so; we would then not bundle the
items, and instead offer a placeholder that links to your page so each lab can enter its
own copy privately.

Could you tell us whether we may include the instrument on these terms, and whether you
would like any particular wording for the attribution? A short reply by email is enough;
we will keep it on file with the software's licence records.

Thank you for your work on this instrument and for considering the request.

With best wishes,

[OWNER NAME]
[ROLE, AFFILIATION]
[EMAIL]
